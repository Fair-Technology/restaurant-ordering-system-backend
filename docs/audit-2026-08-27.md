# Audit — existing code vs the decisions of 2026-08-26/27

Read-only pass over the four repos at `~/FairTech/restaurant-ordering-system`,
checked against every decision made in the design interview.

**Verdict: keep it.** Roughly two thirds of the platform spine is already right,
built on exactly the chosen stack. The restaurant-specific product on top of it
is almost entirely absent, and three subsystems actively contradict decisions
made in the interview and must be reshaped before anything is built on them.

environment exists — five months stale, but it exists.

## Already matches — keep as is

- **Money in whole cents everywhere.** `priceCents`, `lineTotalCents`,
  `monthlyAmountCents`. No decimals in the money path.
- **Menu options are already the right shape.** `Product.variantGroups`
  (sizes, each with a price delta and its own availability) and
  `Product.addonGroups` (paid extras with `minSelectable` / `maxSelectable`).
  This is the one thing that cannot be bolted on later, and it is done.
- **Time-limited availability exists** — `ProductSchedule` with date range,
  daily time window and days of the week. It even carries `offerPrice` and
  `offerLabel`, which is happy-hour pricing already half-built.
- **Opening hours are already the right model** — several windows per weekday,
  plus a separate `closures` list for holidays and one-off shutdowns.
- **Tax is per shop and per product.** `Shop.taxRates` (labelled rates like
  "Standard 19%" / "Reduced 7%") with `Product.taxRateId` pointing at one, plus
  `Shop.countryCode`. The per-country rate table decision is largely built.
- **Currency and timezone are per shop**, not global — the "build these in
  properly now" decision is already satisfied.
- **The slug and its rename rule.** `Shop.slug` is globally unique, and
  `pendingNameChange` plus the `name-change-request/approve` endpoints implement
  exactly the decision that a rename must be requested and approved by the
  platform, not done freely by the owner.
- **A pause switch with a message** (`isPaused`, `pausedMessage`) — very close
  to the "ordering paused" storefront state the order limit needs.
- **A go-live endpoint** (`shops/{id}/go-live-status`) and a Go Live checklist in
  admin — the publish gate, already built.
- **Stripe Connect with direct charges**, an account-session flow for embedded
  onboarding, and both a platform and a Connect webhook.
- **Plan limits are already generic key/value pairs** (`PlanLimit{key,value}`,
  `-1` = unlimited) with per-currency pricing rows and monthly/yearly Stripe
  price ids. This is the entitlement model, ready for new limit keys.
- **Superadmin subscription overrides** with a reason and an expiry — this is
  the "grant a trial by hand" decision, already built.
- **An audit entity** with actor, action, entity, before/after changes, IP and
  user agent.
- **Soft deletes** (`isDeleted`) rather than destructive ones, everywhere.

## Conflicts — built one way, decided another

These are not gaps. Something exists and does the wrong thing; leaving it in
place would silently contradict a decision.

### 1. The usage limit counts products, not orders
`ShopUsage` tracks `activeProductCount`, and `Shop.isDeactivatedDueToLimits` is
set when a shop exceeds its product allowance on cancellation. The decision is
the opposite: **orders per month is the only gate, menu items are unlimited.**
The counter, the reconcile job, the superadmin usage page and the deactivation
rule all invert.

### 2. Staff are Entra users, invited by email
`Shop.members[].userId` is an Entra object id, and there is a whole invitation
subsystem (`invitations/accept`, `invitations/decline`, `users/me/invitations`,
an Invitations page in admin). The decision is that **only the owner uses Entra**
and everyone else is a local account, username and password, created by the
owner, with no email and no self-registration. The invitation flow goes; a local
credential store, a per-restaurant staff login route, and owner-driven password
resets replace it.

### 3. Roles are per-shop and owner-editable
`Shop.roles` holds custom roles with a permission list, and there is full CRUD at
`shops/{id}/roles`. The decision is **three fixed roles — Owner, Manager, Staff —
defined globally in superadmin, not editable by owners.** The per-shop role CRUD
is removed and the permission definition moves up to the platform.

### 4. The order lifecycle is payment-only
`OrderStatus` is `pending_payment | paid | failed | cancelled | refunded` —
nothing about the kitchen. The decision records the full lifecycle: Placed →
Accepted (with a ready-time) → In preparation → Ready / Out for delivery →
Completed, plus Rejected and Cancelled with reasons and timestamps. This is the
single largest reshape in the backend, and everything in the order-intake feature
set depends on it.

### 5. Order acceptance is hardcoded automatic
`Shop.orderAcceptanceMode: 'auto'` is a fixed literal. The decision needs manual
and automatic modes on a per-day time schedule, staff acceptance with a
ready-time, an escalation email, and an auto-reject-with-refund timeout.

### 6. Online payment only
`Shop.paymentPolicy: 'pay_online'`. Cash on collection and cash on delivery are
decided and absent — including the separate "refunded in cash, for the record
only" path.

### 7. The audit log expires
`AuditEntry.ttl` is a Cosmos time-to-live: entries delete themselves. The
decision is **permanent, append-only, with no delete path in any app.** Also
note the entry stores `actorEmail` and `actorName` — the decision was that audit
entries hold identifiers and actions, never personal content, so that a GDPR
erasure can blank an identity table without destroying the trail.

### 8. Customer accounts exist
`Shop.allowGuestCheckout` is a per-shop toggle, and the storefront has a
`MyOrdersPage`. The decision is **no customer accounts at all, ever** — guest
checkout always, order access by an unguessable token link in the confirmation
email. Both come out.

### 9. Branding has four colours
`ShopBranding.colors` carries primary, secondary, tertiary and background. The
decision is **logo, cover image and one accent colour**, with a contrast check
on the accent.

### 10. Minimum order value is per shop
`Shop.minOrderAmountCents` is a single number. The decision puts the minimum
**per delivery postcode**, alongside that postcode's delivery fee.

### 11. Menu content is single-language
`Product.name` and `description` are plain strings. i18next covers the admin
interface only (German and English files exist and are populated). The decision
requires **menu content stored per language**, with the restaurant choosing which
languages it offers and untranslated items falling back to the original text.

### 12. Seeded for Australia
Defaults and examples throughout use `AUD` and `Australia/Sydney`. Germany is
now the first market. Seed data only — the model itself is already
country-agnostic.

## Missing entirely

Grouped by how much each blocks everything else.

**Fulfilment — nothing exists.** There is no collection / delivery / table
concept at all; `Order.orderLocation` is an untyped free-text string. Needed:
fulfilment mode on the order, delivery postcode zones with a fee and a minimum
each, address capture and validation against the zone, out-of-range fallback to
collection, a table list per restaurant, opaque per-table codes, printable QR
sheets, and the generic "scan for menu" code.

**Order intake — nothing exists.** Accept and reject with a ready-time, the
auto-accept schedule, the live order badge over an open connection, the looping
alarm with its shift-start unlock tap, the screen wake-lock, the connection
indicator, email escalation after 3 minutes, and auto-reject with refund after
the timeout.

**Scheduled orders.** Up to 7 days ahead, 15-minute slots, per-slot capacity
caps, a separate "upcoming" list that moves into the live queue one prep-time
before it is due.

**Prep times.** A default per fulfilment mode, the one-tap busy mode, and staff
adjustment at accept. (Per-item prep times are captured but do not drive the
estimate.)

**Food law.** Allergen groups and additive declarations, mandatory before a dish
can be published. `Product.specialInfo` is a loose `{name, icon}` list — a
starting point for dietary tags, not a substitute for a structured allergen
model. Also missing: tax rate bound to the **fulfilment mode**, not only the
dish.

**Invoicing.** Per-restaurant prefix / next number / suffix with placeholders and
padding, forward-only counter, uniqueness on the composed string, number
consumed at issue rather than at order placement, and a PDF receipt attached to
the confirmation email.

**Subscription behaviour.** Warnings at 80 / 90 / 95 percent in-app and by email,
the hard stop with a one-click instant upgrade, downgrade blocked until the shop
is already under the target limits, and the failed-payment path — drop to Basic
with the excess preserved but suspended, after 7 days grace.

**Customer-facing money paths.** Customer self-cancellation until the restaurant
accepts, with automatic refund; full and partial refunds by Owners and Managers
with a reason; the cash "marked refunded" record.

**Growth features.** Discount codes and promotions, and the loyalty engine as
emailed single-use vouchers (count orders against an email address, issue an
expiring code — no balance, no account).

**Legal and GDPR.** A data processing agreement accepted at signup, the
structured-fields Impressum generator, free-text terms and withdrawal policy with
guidance notes, the generated privacy notice naming the restaurant as controller,
the seller-identity statement at checkout, retention and anonymisation rules,
data export on leaving, the notice-and-action route for illegal content, an EU
representative, a sub-processor list, and EU data residency for both Azure and
the Entra tenant.

**Platform plumbing.** Transactional email (provider, sending from the platform
domain with the restaurant as display name and reply-to), `noindex` on
storefronts, link-preview meta injected into the storefront shell by a Function,
the multi-location dashboard, and the five v1 reports.

**Repos.** Playwright lives inside `general-`; the fifth end-to-end repo has not
been split out.

## Where to start

1. **Reshape the order model first** — lifecycle states plus fulfilment mode.
   Everything in intake, scheduling, tax-by-mode and reporting hangs off it, and
   every day it is deferred is another feature built against the wrong shape.
2. **Invert the usage counter** from products to orders. Small, self-contained,
   and it unblocks the whole subscription behaviour set.
3. **Replace the staff and roles subsystem** before any new admin screen is
   built, since every screen depends on who can see it.
4. **Then the design pass** — the audit's own conclusion on Tailwind: the admin
   app has a real Tailwind setup and a populated German and English translation
   file, both worth keeping. Formalise what is there into tokens and a component
   set rather than starting a system from scratch.
